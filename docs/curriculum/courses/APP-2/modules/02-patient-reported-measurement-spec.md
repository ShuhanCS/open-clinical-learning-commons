Module 02 chooses the measure before the course analyzes response, representation, linkage, patient voice, or improvement. The case remains recovery at home after an adult inpatient stay. The work must preserve patient-partner authority and the seven open Module 01 conditions.

The scored component is submitted at the end of Week 2 and frozen into the cumulative Week 3 checkpoint. Module 03 owns the response and representation gate.

## 2. Healthcare decision and named audience

The module decision is:

> Which exact patient-reported measure, item set, scoring rule, language and mode plan, and naming boundary fit the recovery-at-home question well enough to enter a response and representation study?

The primary decision owner is the adult inpatient patient-experience council working with its patient/caregiver advisory group. The decision is about measurement readiness. It does not authorize survey fielding or a clinical workflow change.

Required readers include patient and caregiver partners, the inpatient clinical lead, the patient-experience measurement reviewer, survey operations, language-access and disability-access reviewers, the data steward, and the Module 03 survey-methods reviewer.

## 3. Foundation skill being revisited or extended

### FND-1 extension

Learners reuse exact source identity, field definitions, missing-value handling, reproducible scripts, quality checks, and accessible evidence. APP-2 adds instrument version, response choices, skip logic, scoring direction, question-specific denominators, public-domain and trademark boundaries, language versions, proxy rules, and respondent burden.

### FND-2 extension

Learners reuse estimand and validity reasoning but do not fit a predictive model. They separate construct validity, content validity, scoring reproducibility, internal consistency, aggregate-score reliability, and meaningful interpretation. A correct calculation cannot rescue the wrong construct or a changed instrument.

### DA-730 use

Learners communicate the scoring result with exact tables and structured text. The module does not repeat visual-encoding instruction.

## 4. Learning outcomes that can be assessed

By completing Module 02, the learner can:

1. verify the current HCAHPS QAG V19.0 survey suite and April 2026 addendum;
2. distinguish the legacy 29-question instrument from the updated 32-question instrument;
3. select the updated Q22 and Q23 Discharge Information pair for the recovery-at-home process question;
4. keep updated Q20 Information About Symptoms separate from the Q22/Q23 composite;
5. apply Q21 discharge-destination skip logic without recoding not applicable as no;
6. distinguish a PREM from a PROM, satisfaction item, clinical outcome, and patient-partnership record;
7. compare candidate item sets using construct fit, content coverage, burden, language, mode, rights, and actionability;
8. calculate question-level top-box proportions with question-specific denominators;
9. calculate the unadjusted teaching composite as the mean of question-level proportions;
10. explain why a person-weighted item mean is a different calculation;
11. explain why rounded adjusted public item percentages cannot reproduce the official CMS composite exactly;
12. calculate a two-item teaching reliability diagnostic without treating synthetic output as instrument validation;
13. distinguish internal consistency from the HCAHPS hospital-level reliability target;
14. refuse an unsupported meaningful-change threshold;
15. audit nine mail languages, nine web languages, and four phone-script languages;
16. state proxy, low-literacy, disability-access, non-digital, burden, and translation conditions;
17. apply the public-domain, attribution, CAHPS naming, modification, and comparison rules;
18. produce a reproducible 20-point measurement record with complete source and agent disclosure; and
19. issue a bounded Module 03 progression decision.

### Synthetic patient-level scoring fixture

The public file contains no patient-level responses. The module therefore creates a deterministic 240-row synthetic fixture solely to teach response coding, skip logic, item-specific denominators, composite calculation, and reliability diagnostics.

## 10. Worked example and instructor walkthrough

The instructor begins with a flawed calculation:

> Average each hospital's two published item percentages, call that value the official HCAHPS Discharge Information score, and use the largest difference to identify a low-performing hospital.

Learners identify the defects:

1. public item percentages are rounded and adjusted;
2. the official composite is produced under CMS rules and may not equal a simple average of the published values;
3. item-specific denominators are hidden in the public aggregate file;
4. the reporting period spans a survey-version transition;
5. the file contains no patient-level responses;
6. a difference is not evidence of poor care or a target for intervention; and
7. named hospital ranking is outside the module decision.

The corrected workflow reproduces an unadjusted teaching composite from synthetic item responses, quantifies the published item-mean difference without ranking, and uses the official CMS composite for any public reporting statement.

## 16. Common errors, failure modes, and instructor interventions

- Calling Q22/Q23 a PROM or clinical outcome: return to the construct comparison.
- Adding Q20 to the Discharge Information composite: return to the official public-measure crosswalk.
- Treating another-facility responses as no: return to Q21 skip logic.
- Dividing both items by one shared denominator: inspect question-specific missingness.
- Using a person mean as the official composite: reproduce both formulas and name them correctly.
- Averaging public item percentages and calling the result official: compare it with `H_COMP_6_Y_P` and retain the CMS value.
- Calling synthetic alpha validation evidence: label it a procedural diagnostic only.
- Confusing the 0.8 aggregate reliability target with internal consistency: return to QAG sampling guidance.
- Assigning meaningful change from one cross section: record that no threshold is established.
- Assuming nine written languages solve disability or low-literacy access: complete the access audit.
- Calling a partial local survey HCAHPS: apply the naming decision.
- Presenting agent output as source confirmation: require the source file, calculation, or executable check.

## 17. Accessibility, equity, privacy, and responsible-claim checks

The package preserves every official language and mode file currently published by HCAHPS. Availability is not proof that a local respondent received an accessible option. Module 03 must still measure offer, use, response, and missingness by prespecified access routes.

The learner must address large print, screen reader compatibility, telephone access, non-digital access, low literacy, supported proxy administration, patient permission, language preference, translation review, and survey burden. A patient partner may require revision or referral when wording or access does not fit the intended population.

The public data contain facility identity but no patient rows. The synthetic file contains no real people. No protected or restricted data enter the package or an external agent.

HCAHPS measures patient experience. It does not prove satisfaction, health outcome, quality, causation, or intervention benefit. A partial local survey produces unofficial local evidence and cannot be benchmarked against official adjusted CMS results.

## 19. Answer key and instructor notes

The reference selection is the unchanged updated HCAHPS Q22/Q23 Discharge Information item pair. Q20 is retained as a separate caregiver-information item and is not added to the composite.

For a short local feedback process, the item pair must be labeled as public-domain HCAHPS-derived items rather than an official HCAHPS survey. The local result is unadjusted and unofficial. It cannot be compared with official CMS hospital scores. A program seeking official HCAHPS administration must follow the complete QAG and approved participation path.

The synthetic exercise establishes that question-level and person-level weighting can differ. It does not establish reliability, validity, or meaningful change for a real population. The public concordance exercise establishes that rounded adjusted item percentages do not reproduce every official composite exactly.

The expected progression is `continue with conditions`. Module 03 may construct the response and representation package. Survey fielding, hospital ranking, clinical action, and official benchmarking remain prohibited.
