## 1. Module identity and place in the course

- Module ID: `oclc-app2-07`.
- Version: `0.1.0`.

Module 07 turns the accepted measurement, response, linked-evidence, patient-voice, equity, partnership, improvement, and model work into an accountable leadership decision. It adds no new retrospective analysis and cannot repair a failed upstream gate.

## 2. Decision, readers, and allowed recommendations

The leadership panel must answer:

> What should the service and patient advisory group do next, who owns it, how will patients hear what happened, and when must the proposal stop or change?

The primary receivers are an adult inpatient clinical decision owner and a patient/caregiver advisory group. The panel also includes the APP-2 faculty owner, survey and measurement leads, improvement and operations leads, an access and language lead, an equity and accessibility reviewer, a governance reviewer, and an independent methods reviewer.

Allowed recommendations are:

1. `run bounded prospective measurement and improvement test`;
2. `revise before testing`;
3. `refer`; or
4. `stop`.

The reference recommendation is `revise before testing`. Package acceptance and a recommendation to test are separate decisions.

## 4. Patient or caregiver co-lead

A patient or caregiver co-lead shares authority over the partnership, communication, burden, access, feedback, disagreement, and accountability parts of the block. The role cannot be filled by synthetic comments, a simulated partner record, a clinician speaking for patients, or an agent.

Before alpha, the program must confirm the person's name, compensation, preparation, access needs, language and format needs, schedule, decision rights, attribution choice, recording consent, withdrawal route, disagreement rights, and final review. The construction reference tests the required records but contains no actual patient or caregiver statement.

If the co-lead has not been confirmed, curriculum construction may continue with the role marked pending. The module cannot be advertised or delivered as patient co-led.

## 5. Ownership and claim boundary

The clinical decision owner can accept, narrow, revise, refer, stop, or request a bounded local protocol. The patient/caregiver co-lead can require language changes, add burden or access questions, retain disagreement, reject claims presented as patient voice, and withhold partnership approval.

Module 07 cannot:

- field a survey or contact a patient;
- modify or call a local item validated;
- publish an official HCAHPS result;
- present generated comments as testimony;
- infer preference from channel use;
- rank a hospital, clinician, group, or community;
- claim inequity, discrimination, cause, fairness, feasibility, safety, efficacy, or benefit from the teaching evidence;
- target a patient or group with a model; or
- authorize clinical implementation or model deployment.

## 6. Assessable learning outcomes

By the end of Module 07, learners can:

1. trace a leadership claim to the accepted Week 3 or Week 6 evidence;
2. separate public, public-derived, synthetic, simulated, and proposed evidence;
3. explain whose voice is present, missing, generated, or still required;
4. make one allowed recommendation with a clear authorization boundary;
5. assign shared clinical, patient, operational, analytic, access, and governance ownership;
6. write a patient-facing summary that does not overstate evidence;
7. define a universal-offer prospective measurement and improvement protocol;
8. preserve support, suppression, missingness, uncertainty, and disagreement;
9. define monitoring, feedback, accountability, stop, escalation, and reassessment rules;
10. explain why the bounded random forest does not change the adjustment decision; and
11. defend the package before clinician, patient, operations, methods, and governance reviewers.

## 13. Recommendation and bounded prospective protocol

`leadership-recommendation.md` must state one allowed recommendation, the shared decision owners, current authorization, evidence basis, unknowns, patient-partner status, model decision, required revisions, and next review.

The bounded protocol must define local eligibility, universal offer, no model targeting, unchanged accepted Q22 and Q23 teaching items, proposed local clarity and burden questions kept separate, comparison plan, run-in, duration, consent and contact choices, language and format routes, proxy and interpreter support, data classes, analysis plan, feedback route, review cadence, and final authority.

The protocol remains a design proposal until local and human review authorize it.

## 14. Patient-facing communication and feedback

The patient-facing summary must explain in plain language:

- what the teaching project studied;
- what information came from public data;
- what information was generated for teaching;
- what remains unknown;
- what the team is considering;
- what patients and caregivers can change or reject;
- how burden, access, privacy, and no-contact choices are protected; and
- how results and reasons will return to patients.

It cannot include generated comments as quotes, unsupported group claims, official HCAHPS language, or a promise of benefit. A patient/caregiver co-lead must approve the final language before alpha.

## 15. Partnership, equity, access, and shared authority

The partner decision record must preserve the simulated-status label until a named person participates. It records compensation, access, preparation, data and recording choices, attribution, authority, disagreements, requested changes, team responses, final review, and withdrawal.

Group evidence retains source categories, counts, uncertainty, support, suppression, and blank unsupported results. Access routes include phone, mail, web, interpreter-supported, proxy-supported, accessible formats, and no contact. Channel use does not equal preference, and universal offer does not prove equal access.

## 16. Measures, monitoring, feedback, and accountability

The package retains 14 prospective measures across implementation, process, response, outcome, access, balancing, accountability, and safety. Each measure has a numerator, denominator, exclusions, timing, owner, missingness rule, interpretation, trigger, and response.

Review begins with counts, missingness, reach, burden, unwanted contact, safety routing, and access. Q22 and Q23 results remain teaching measures, not official adjusted HCAHPS reporting. Supported group review follows prespecified rules without targeting or ranking.

Every review cycle produces a dated, accessible response stating what changed, what did not change, why, who owns the next step, and what remains open. Failure to return results is an accountability failure that can stop progression.

## 17. Stop, escalation, rollback, and reassessment

Stop or pause rules must cover unsafe routing, unwanted contact, missing access routes, partner withdrawal or rejection, invalid denominators, low data completeness, unsupported group inference, privacy or consent failure, burden above the agreed limit, capacity failure, official-reporting confusion, unauthorized targeting, and loss of accountable ownership.

Each rule names the trigger, immediate action, escalation owner, evidence needed to restart, and who shares restart authority. A serious safety, privacy, consent, or partnership failure stops the proposal rather than merely generating a note.

## 18. Accessibility, responsible agent use, and human accountability
