## 3. Learning outcomes

By the end of the module, a learner can:

1. acquire a complete official public geometry release from a direct source URL and verify its byte count, SHA-256, and member inventory;
2. distinguish tract keys and boundary geometry from demographic, clinical, access, community, priority, and eligibility evidence;
3. validate geographic keys, state and county identity, CRS, geometry type, nulls, empties, validity, projected area, and display generalization;
4. join a complete geometry universe to an accepted public measure without numeric key coercion, silent row loss, duplicate expansion, or zero-filling unavailable values;
5. retain the modeled estimate, interval, population field, release identity, support state, and claim limit in an exact tract table;
6. apply and explain a declared classroom support-review rule without presenting it as a source quality designation or exclusion rule;
7. calculate a population-weighted county teaching summary and distinguish it from an official county PLACES estimate;
8. explain how tract and county aggregation can change a displayed pattern without claiming that one geographic scale is inherently correct;
9. design one accessible static map using declared absolute classes, a named unavailable state, a non-color support cue, a source note, an exact table, and a text alternative;
10. identify and rewrite ecological, compositional, causal, stigmatizing, community, and action claims that the map cannot support;
11. reproduce the release from frozen sources and reject material mutations; and
12. make a bounded progression decision that keeps ranking, targeting, eligibility, outreach, funding, implementation, production, and deployment prohibited.

## 15. Ecological, contextual, and non-stigmatizing claim contract

The claims audit tests ten statements. It accepts two bounded statements and rejects eight unsupported interpretations.

Accepted language may state that the map displays 1,597 source-labeled public modeled estimates in declared absolute classes, retains 23 tracts as unavailable, marks 49 classroom review states, and shows 645 tract class changes under a county teaching summary.

Rejected language includes:

- adults in darker tracts have diabetes;
- the map shows observed cases;
- gray means zero;
- darker classes should be prioritized;
- county teaching summaries are official county PLACES estimates;
- hatching is a CDC unreliability label;
- residents share the tract estimate; and
- the map identifies communities that need intervention.

Area context and resident characteristics are not interchangeable. A tract estimate cannot describe every resident, establish why a pattern exists, or identify which people would benefit from a program. A map cannot stand in for access, capacity, burden, benefit, harm, competing priorities, community voice, jurisdiction, or a responsible owner.

Non-stigmatizing language describes the source, measure, geography, uncertainty, missing evidence, and accountable decision. It does not call a place failing, deficient, noncompliant, problematic, vulnerable, high need, or priority based on a class.

## 20. Accessibility, equity, privacy, AI, versioning, and review

It contains no protected, identifiable, address-level, encounter-level, or patient-level data. An agent may not fabricate community input or convert a map pattern into a target.
