## 3. Learning outcomes

By the end of the module, a learner can:

1. distinguish a policy rule from a data fact, prediction, map, or fairness result;
2. define a fixed resource constraint before comparing alternatives;
3. keep modeled public evidence separate from fictional capacity, access, review, and burden assumptions;
4. express an equal geographic rule, need-based rule, capacity-aware rule, and community-review rule in inspectable terms;
5. name the narrow fairness definition used by each rule and the value it leaves unprotected;
6. reproduce every included and excluded tract under an exact tie breaker;
7. audit access, capacity, travel, staff readiness, burden, review state, unresolved questions, and objection for each result;
8. measure geographic concentration without relabeling a county as needy, underserved, ready, or entitled;
9. audit synthetic marginal group coverage while leaving suppressed cells unavailable and dimensions separate;
10. compare benefit, harm, burden, nonaction, balancing measures, appeal, correction, pause, and stop conditions;
11. run predeclared sensitivity variants without searching for a preferred answer;
12. recognize when a resource or concentration constraint cannot be filled;
13. use AI assistance only for bounded drafting or code that receives independent verification; and
14. make a progression decision that carries a fictional planning candidate forward without giving it automatic or real authority.

## 8. Four-rule definition contract

### Equal geographic rule

Fairness means equal county representation. The rule selects two teaching areas per county using a source-independent SHA-256 tie breaker. It reaches all 14 counties. It does not account for county population, public estimate, access, capacity, staff, review, objection, or burden.

### Need-based rule

Fairness means greater fictional attention to higher modeled area-level prevalence. The rule orders by modeled prevalence, interval width, and a stable tie breaker. It retains limited-support rows rather than hiding them. The rule is deliberately inspectable, but a modeled estimate cannot become an automatic need score.

### Capacity-aware rule

Fairness means feasible fictional delivery under the fixed resource constraint. The rule requires at least ten fictional capacity places and orders by capacity, staff readiness, access readiness, burden, travel, and a stable tie breaker. It can favor places that already have more delivery assets.

### Community-review rule

Fairness means procedural readiness with recourse. The rule requires ready fictional review, no unresolved objection, language and disability access readiness, and at least ten fictional capacity places. It orders by unresolved questions, burden, capacity, modeled context, and a stable tie breaker, with no more than three awards per county.

Every rule definition publishes its candidate filter, selection order, tie breaker, award size, resource total, fairness definition, tradeoff, and no-action limit. No hidden fifth rule or optimization routine is permitted.

## 12. Fairness-definition and tradeoff contract

The module does not ask whether a rule is fair in the abstract. It asks what the rule protects, who pays for that choice, and which facts or rights remain missing.

- Equal geographic fairness protects county representation and ignores unequal population, access, capacity, review, and burden.
- Need-based fairness gives more classroom attention to higher modeled prevalence and risks turning uncertain area context into entitlement or priority.
- Capacity-aware fairness protects deliverability and risks rewarding places that already possess more capacity.
- Community-review fairness protects a declared procedural filter and risks delaying or excluding areas with incomplete review, while leaving staff and travel problems unresolved.

Average possible benefit cannot compensate for concentrated burden, inaccessible delivery, unresolved objection, suppressed group evidence, or an absent appeal route. A rule can be transparent and still be unacceptable.

The accepted phrasing is that the community-review comparator is the least unacceptable fictional planning candidate under the declared conditions. The release does not call it the fairest rule.

## 16. Sensitivity and constraint-failure contract

The release contains 20 predeclared variants, five per rule. It tests resource quantity, tie direction, county quota, modeled lower confidence limit, support handling, access readiness, capacity threshold, community-review readiness, and county maximum.

Eighteen variants fill the requested awards. Two expose a shortfall:

- the expanded community-review rule fills 38 of 42 awards under the three-per-county maximum; and
- the stricter two-per-county community variant fills 26 of 28 awards.

Reversing the equal geographic tie breaker replaces all 28 base selections. Removing its county quota retains 15 and reduces county coverage from 14 to nine. The need-based supported-only variant retains two of 28 and replaces 26. The tested capacity variants retain the same 28 selections. Allowing community rows marked `needs_revision` changes nine selections.

Sensitivity is descriptive. The module cannot tune assumptions until a preferred result appears. A shortfall is an answer: the declared rule and constraint cannot allocate the requested fictional resource.
