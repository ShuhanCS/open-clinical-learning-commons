The Module 06 freezer builds and validates the complete Module 05 reference workspace. The frozen payload contains 340 files. The outer handoff manifest contains 340 sorted rows, is 76,008 bytes, and has SHA-256 `0ab8cc15d252ef91436aa1b281f316e4eb21115aefc668a0930d04c90397a828`.

## 3. Learning outcomes

By the end of the module, a learner can:

1. distinguish curriculum-package readiness from intervention readiness and real-world authority;
2. turn a transparent planning candidate into an inspectable fictional intervention contract;
3. define an intended population without inferring individual need or eligibility from area evidence;
4. write a theory of change that exposes assumptions, failure signals, owners, and claim limits;
5. map a delivery pathway with every human decision, access check, feedback route, pause condition, and stop right;
6. define implementation, process, access, choice, burden, feedback, safety, governance, and availability measures;
7. preserve numerator, denominator, unavailable state, threshold origin, cadence, owner, and human response for every measure;
8. interpret a monitoring dry run as a software and governance test rather than a service or outcome study;
9. route questions, correction, refusal, withdrawal, appeal, disagreement, objection, incident, pause, revision, stop, and retirement;
10. draft a later causal evaluation proposal without manufacturing a current effect estimate;
11. freeze an unsupervised model question, matrix, features, transformations, scaling, cluster count, seed, algorithm, and stability rule before seeing results;
12. use adjusted Rand agreement, cluster size, geographic support, and selected-area coverage to test a clustering challenger;
13. reject an ML result without tuning it after inspection;
14. preserve a transparent decision rule when an ML challenger contributes no accepted use;
15. document AI assistance without transferring community, clinical, causal, or progression authority; and
16. make a progression decision that carries earlier points once and keeps Module 07 behind the separate Week 6 checkpoint.

## 5. Continuing evidence thread and public-source authority

The case remains `FMA-DP-01`, an explicitly fictional Massachusetts adult diabetes-prevention planning review. The accepted universe contains 1,597 real tract identifiers with a CDC PLACES modeled crude adult diabetes prevalence estimate and interval.

The public estimate remains area-level modeled context. It does not provide observed cases, personal risk, consent, preference, program capacity, access, staffing, cost, benefit, harm, service use, or intervention outcome truth.

Module 06 reads the accepted linked candidate table rather than returning to the public endpoint. The source authority and exact release identity remain inside the frozen handoff. Public prevalence is used as one descriptive area-profile feature only. It cannot generate synthetic response, access, burden, incident, feedback, or participation states.

The source separation matters twice. First, it prevents an area estimate from quietly becoming a fictional intervention outcome. Second, it prevents the cluster model from being described as a personal diabetes-risk model.

## 7. Intervention and authority contract

The candidate is a fictional voluntary diabetes-prevention access and navigation program. Its proposed elements are accessible program information, voluntary response, navigation, accessible scheduling, group or remote prevention support, feedback, incident review, and named pause and stop rights.

The intended population is adults represented through accepted area-level planning evidence. The contract does not identify a person, determine eligibility, infer diabetes status, contact anyone, enroll anyone, or deliver care.

The starting resource remains fixed:

- 28 carried tract rows;
- 10 fictional places per row;
- 280 fictional places total;
- no partial place;
- no reserve or hidden reallocation; and
- no cluster-based change.

Human owners cover clinical population framing, program operations, language access, disability access, community accountability, safety, privacy, monitoring, evaluation methods, and program stewardship. The program steward retains pause, revision, stop, and retirement authority.

No threshold, model, score, map, generated feedback state, or AI output may initiate real contact, eligibility, allocation, enrollment, service, punishment, implementation, or deployment.

## 8. Theory of change and delivery pathway

The theory of change contains seven steps from accessible information through a later causal evaluation proposal. Each step names an activity, a hoped-for near-term change, an assumption, a failure signal, an owner, and a claim limit.

The ten-step pathway makes every transition inspectable:

1. carry the transparent fictional candidate;
2. review staff, travel, burden, and access readiness;
3. prepare accessible information;
4. process or hold a fictional offer test;
5. preserve response, refusal, and ambiguity;
6. test accessible scheduling and modality;
7. test attendance, completion, fidelity, and burden states;
8. route feedback, objection, and incidents;
9. review all monitoring measures; and
10. defer effect estimation to APP-6.

A theory arrow is not evidence that change occurred. A completed dry-run path is not a completed clinical pathway. The learner must keep the desired mechanism, the synthetic test, and any later observed implementation distinct.

## 10. Monitoring registry and threshold contract

Twenty predeclared measures cover readiness, access, reach, choice, delivery, implementation, balancing, feedback, recourse, safety, governance, outcome availability, and real-world authority.

Every measure declares:

- numerator and denominator;
- cadence;
- source;
- owner;
- unavailable state;
- higher, lower, or descriptive direction;
- teaching threshold;
- threshold origin;
- human response;
- no automatic action; and
- interpretation limit.

Thresholds are classroom triggers. They are not externally validated clinical standards. A passed measure does not prove access, quality, benefit, safety, effectiveness, or fairness. A triggered measure creates human review, correction, pause, or stop work. It does not create automatic outreach, enrollment, allocation, service, or punishment.

An unavailable denominator stays unavailable. It cannot become zero or disappear inside an average. In the reference run all 20 denominators are available, but the validator still preserves the unavailable-state contract.

## 12. Feedback, recourse, incident, and stewardship contract

The dry run contains 165 no-feedback states, 53 supportive states, 48 questions, and 14 objections. An objection creates a hold and community review. It cannot become a negative personal or place label.

The 23 incident tests include seven access failures, five privacy near misses, and eleven scheduling failures. Every incident has a named route. Access failures and privacy near misses pause the affected path. Scheduling failures create operations review without automatic punishment or exclusion.

The release preserves:

- accessible question and correction routes;
- refusal and withdrawal without penalty;
- appeal and disagreement;
- objection and community review;
- incident investigation;
- correction and retest;
- pause and stop authority;
- revision with versioned evidence; and
- retirement when assumptions, access, safety, community support, or stewardship fail.

Silence is not consent. No-response is not refusal. Generated feedback is not community participation.

## 13. Later evaluation and causal boundary

The learner writes a prospective evaluation proposal so intervention design and causal design are connected without being conflated.

The proposal must name a causal question, contrast, assignment mechanism, outcomes, follow-up, missing-data plan, interference assumptions, equity estimands, balancing measures, and stopping rules. It must also state which ethical, clinical, community, operational, privacy, and governance approvals would be required.

Module 06 estimates no effect. The dry run has no participants and no outcomes. CDC PLACES modeled prevalence is not an intervention outcome. Attendance, completion, fidelity, access, burden, objection, and incident test states are generated software states.

APP-6 owns any later causal identification and effect estimation. A learner who reports benefit, effectiveness, improvement, harm reduction, or a causal effect from Module 06 fails the claim gate.

## 14. Fixed clustering challenger contract

The challenger asks whether area-profile groupings support bounded descriptive tailoring questions. It does not ask who needs a program or where resources should go.

The matrix has all 1,597 accepted candidate rows. Selection labels are excluded. The nine fixed features are:

1. modeled crude prevalence percent;
2. modeled interval width in percentage points;
3. log1p adult population field;
4. fictional capacity places;
5. fictional travel minutes;
6. fictional delivery burden score;
7. fictional language-access readiness;
8. fictional disability-access readiness; and
9. fictional staff-ready indicator.

Missing values fail the build. No imputation is allowed.

The base model is scikit-learn 1.9.0 `KMeans` with four clusters, standard scaling, `random_state=73056`, `n_init=20`, and the Lloyd algorithm. Four alternate seeds use the same base transformation and scaling. Robust, min-max, and unit-norm scaling provide three fixed scaling challengers.

No parameter may be changed after seeing the result. Cluster labels cannot rank, select, exclude, allocate, infer individual traits, define fairness, replace the transparent rule, bypass review, or enter deployment.

## 15. Cluster support, stability, and accepted result

The base solution has four nonempty clusters. The smallest contains 267 tracts, above the minimum of 80. The four alternate seeds have adjusted Rand agreement values from 0.894 to 0.986, so the seed standard passes.

The scaling variants show that the grouping depends strongly on representation choice:

| Variant | Adjusted Rand vs base | Smallest cluster | Carried-row clusters |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Robust scaling | 0.120 | 218 | 4 |
| Min-max scaling | 0.507 | 287 | 2 |
| Unit-norm scaling | 0.005 | 150 | 3 |

The scaling-variant median adjusted Rand value is 0.120, below the fixed 0.60 standard. The 28 carried rows span only two base clusters, below the required three-cluster coverage.

The challenger therefore fails. It is not useful for accepted tailoring questions. The release keeps the feature matrix, assignments, profiles, geography, selected-row review, and stability table so the rejection is reproducible.

The model is not tuned, rescued, relabeled as exploratory success, or used to change the transparent comparison.
