## 5. Assessable learning outcomes

By completing Module 07, learners can:

1. state the clinical performance decision and evidence boundary in clinical language;
2. distinguish a curriculum-package decision from a clinical recommendation;
3. explain the strongest accepted evidence and the condition that most narrows it;
4. explain why a process signal does not establish cause;
5. explain the forecast, empirical range, difficult periods, and capacity limits;
6. explain why no scenario qualified and retain failed evidence;
7. interpret feasibility status without treating a local-evidence gap as favorable;
8. explain the monitoring dashboard and human escalation boundary;
9. explain why the ML challenger does not replace the transparent forecast;
10. identify people who may benefit, carry burden, be excluded, or face safety or access risk;
11. assign accountable, responsible, consulted, and informed roles;
12. define a revision and learning path without silently starting a test;
13. preserve disagreement and state who may pause, refer, or stop the work;
14. provide equivalent access to every decision-relevant fact; and
15. defend a specific recommendation with evidence, practical meaning, and limits.

## 16. Accessibility, reproducibility, and responsible claims

Every decision-relevant display has an exact table and equivalent text. Briefs use plain clinical language and define technical terms in place. Color is not the only carrier of meaning. A live, recorded, written, or supported oral defense may be used if the same 14 questions, evidence standard, and follow-up challenge are preserved.

The responsible-claims audit tests synthetic-as-real language, cause, safety, staffing, productivity, equity, forecast certainty, scenario effect, feasibility, ML acceptance, automation, testing, implementation, and deployment claims.

Agent output is not evidence. Each material contribution needs an exact accepted path, a different verification route, a correction record, and a named human owner. No patient, workplace, restricted, identifiable, secret, credential, or identity-expanding data may enter an external agent.
