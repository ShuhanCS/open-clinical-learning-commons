## Recommendation and revision contract

Allowed recommendations are:

- `run bounded prospective improvement test`;
- `revise before testing`;
- `refer`; or
- `stop`.

The reference recommendation is `revise before testing`. No current scenario can become the test protocol. A revision plan may specify the evidence and governance needed to create a new scenario contract, but it cannot lower a failed rule, hide a stress result, impute an unavailable outcome, or start a test.

## People, safety, access, and workforce contract

The module identifies who may experience benefit, delay, burden, exclusion, extra work, blame, or risk. It covers high-acuity patients, language and mobility support, safety-event review, follow-up completeness, frontline staff, staffing and scheduling owners, and data stewards.

Synthetic differences and simulated outputs retain questions. They do not establish real inequity, safety, workload, feasibility, cause, or benefit.

## Monitoring and stewardship contract

The 12 accepted measures retain their exact definitions, evidence states, owners, cadence, thresholds, unavailable rules, and claim limits. The ten escalation rules retain human confirmation, decision ownership, safeguards, no-change fallback, documentation, restart requirements, and zero automatic actions.

Leadership adds decision governance and communication. It does not change the upstream measure or threshold values.

## Communication contract

The frontline brief explains the fictional case, what was found, what remains unknown, why no option is ready, what no-change monitoring means, and how staff can raise burden, safety, access, and disagreement concerns. It avoids blame and does not promise a test.

The leadership summary states the decision, evidence, recommendation, owners, conditions, next review, and absent authority in one concise clinical brief.
