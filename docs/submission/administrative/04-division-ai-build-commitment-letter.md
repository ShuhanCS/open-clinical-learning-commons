NIH Integration of Nutrition Training into Health Care Education Challenge

Re: Division of Artificial Intelligence commitment for Open Nutrition Data Commons for Emergency Medicine Residency

To the Challenge Reviewers:

The Mass General Brigham Department of Emergency Medicine Division of Artificial Intelligence supports the proposed Developing Track curriculum, Open Nutrition Data Commons for Emergency Medicine Residency.

Subject to institutional approval, the Division will lead curriculum engineering and technical coordination in partnership with HAEMR, the Mass General Brigham University Healthcare Data Analytics Program, and a qualified nutrition professional. The working curriculum maps 16 HHS competencies to 40 hours of emergency medicine residency education.

The Division commits to:

- build reusable case, data, visualization, simulation, and assessment materials for the selected competencies;
- use synthetic patient cases and appropriately licensed open data for public teaching assets;
- create a responsible AI exercise in which residents verify sources, identify unsupported or unsafe nutrition claims, and document the final human decision;
- maintain reproducible build records, source provenance, known limits, accessibility notes, and versioned public releases; and
- prepare an adoption package that another residency can use without MGB credentials, protected health information, MGB Azure access, or proprietary software.

MGB's institutionally procured Azure environment may support internal development where approved. It will not be a prerequisite for public adoption. The proposed curriculum does not place an AI system into clinical care and does not authorize patient-facing AI use.

The Division also supports Shuhan He, MD, as the proposed institutional Point of Contact, subject to written Mass General Brigham authorization.
