# Instructor notes

## Teaching purpose

The point is not to find the best score. Learners should see that each transparent rule protects one value and creates a different burden. Equal geography ignores readiness. Modeled-prevalence ordering concentrates uncertainty and access problems. Capacity favors places that already have capacity. Procedural readiness can delay or exclude places whose review is incomplete.

Keep the public and synthetic layers separate in discussion. The public values are accepted modeled area-level estimates. The planning council, resource constraint, capacity, access, staff, review, objection, burden, and decisions are fictional. A generated review state is not consent.

## Suggested sequence

1. Freeze and inspect the Module 04 handoff.
2. Reproduce the fictional source and verify that its fields do not depend on public prevalence.
3. Read the fixed resource contract and four rule definitions before looking at results.
4. Reconcile all 1,597 candidate rows and 6,388 assignments.
5. Compare inclusion, exclusion, access, capacity, burden, geography, group support, benefit, and harm.
6. Run the predeclared sensitivities and identify arbitrary or fragile choices.
7. Draft the community-review and recourse record before recommending progression.
8. Defend why a rule may enter planning without receiving real authority.

## Discussion prompts

- What does each rule mean by fairness?
- Who is excluded or delayed by the rule, and why?
- Which consequences are visible only because the synthetic layer exists?
- What cannot be learned because group cells are suppressed or marginal?
- When does capacity-aware planning reward historic advantage?
- What must a review process collect before anyone can claim consent or feasibility?
- Which sensitivity result should force a pause?
