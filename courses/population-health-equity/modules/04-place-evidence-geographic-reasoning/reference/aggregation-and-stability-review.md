# Aggregation and small-area stability review

## Display classes

The map uses fixed absolute percentage-point classes rather than ranks or quantiles.

| Class | Tracts |
|---|---:|
| Less than 5.0% | 82 |
| 5.0% to less than 10.0% | 826 |
| 10.0% to less than 15.0% | 608 |
| 15.0% to less than 20.0% | 64 |
| 20.0% or greater | 17 |
| Unavailable | 23 |

The 1,597 mapped values range from 0.7% to 30.5%. The retained PLACES population field sums to 5,663,670. These facts describe the accepted modeled public release. They do not establish observed counts, individual risk, a real disparity, need, vulnerability, or an action rule.

## Classroom support review

The classroom limited-support rule is triggered when the modeled interval width is at least 4.0 percentage points or the retained PLACES population field is below 500.

- Supported for teaching display: `1,548` tracts.
- Interval-width trigger only: `43` tracts.
- Population trigger only: `5` tracts.
- Both triggers: `1` tract.
- Total limited-support review: `49` tracts.
- Unavailable: `23` tracts.

The rule is not a CDC quality designation, suppression rule, clinical threshold, or exclusion rule. The map retains the estimate and uses hatching as a prompt to read the exact interval and population field.

## Aggregation comparison

The module creates 14 population-weighted county teaching summaries from the accepted tract estimates. They are not official county PLACES estimates. The tract-to-county comparison contains 1,597 rows. A total of 645 tracts move to a different display class when compared with the county summary class.

This is evidence that aggregation can change the displayed pattern. It does not prove that tract geography is better, that county geography is better, or that one reveals a true individual or community condition. The geography must match the decision question, data support, and accountable audience.

## Decision

The exact values, interval review, and aggregation comparison are accepted for geographic reasoning. No output may use a county or tract class as a rank, priority label, target, eligibility rule, outreach list, or funding input.
