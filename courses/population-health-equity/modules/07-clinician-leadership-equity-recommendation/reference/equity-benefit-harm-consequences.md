# Equity, benefit, harm, and consequence review

The four fictional rules are equal geographic, need based, capacity aware, and community review. They use one fixed resource contract but distribute possible benefit, burden, access, exclusion, and uncertainty differently.

The community-review comparison reaches 11 counties and clears the declared limited-support, language-access, disability-access, and unresolved-objection checks. It still contains 12 high-travel selections and one high-burden selection. It is the least unacceptable option under the declared classroom contract. It is not proven fair, community endorsed, operationally ready, or generalizable.

Possible benefit includes accessible prevention information and navigation if a later human process establishes real need, consent, capacity, and value. Possible harm includes stigma, ecological misclassification, exclusion, travel, time, administrative work, false reassurance, distrust, privacy loss, diverted resources, and treating community review as ceremonial approval.

Fairness cannot be reduced to one score. Leadership must show which definition is used, who gains, who bears burden, what evidence is missing, who may disagree, and who can stop the process.
