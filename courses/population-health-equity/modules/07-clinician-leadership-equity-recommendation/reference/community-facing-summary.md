# Community-facing summary

## What this classroom project asks

This project asks whether a fictional planning council should consider a carefully governed conversation about a possible diabetes-prevention information and navigation program. It does not start a program.

The public data describe areas, not individual people. No person is being classified or contacted. The program details, staffing, capacity, monitoring events, and resource limits are fictional teaching material.

The evidence shows that different planning rules place possible benefits and burdens in different locations. Some selected areas still have staffing, travel, or delivery concerns, and outcomes are unavailable, so the project cannot say that the program works. A clustering method was rejected because its result changed too much under different scaling and did not cover enough of the selected areas.

The proposal is not ready for implementation. No outreach, eligibility, funding, allocation, enrollment, or service is approved.

If an authorized review were later considered, affected people and organizations would need meaningful rights to correct the evidence, disagree, refuse, request language or disability access, raise harm without retaliation, and pause or stop the process. Their participation or silence could not be treated as approval.
