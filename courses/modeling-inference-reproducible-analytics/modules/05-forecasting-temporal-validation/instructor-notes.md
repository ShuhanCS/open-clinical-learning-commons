# Instructor notes

## Teaching purpose

The central habit is simple: the future may evaluate the past, but it may not train it. Ask learners to defend target, cutoff, and benchmarks before discussing a package or model name.

## Sequence

1. Reconcile one target row from the three source admission counts.
2. Draw all five folds and label which rows a fit may see.
3. Hand-calculate `LAST` and `SNAIVE52` for one target.
4. Reveal the damped-Holt aggregate result only after benchmark predictions exist.
5. Compare F04 and F05 before accepting the aggregate ranking.
6. Contrast source-unit errors with low-denominator MAPE.
7. Use the enormous interval widths to discuss misspecification and calibration.
8. Read ADF and ARIMA tables as recognition, not a recipe.
9. End with source coverage, referrals, and the nonoperational boundary.

## Misconceptions to probe

- Time-ordered data do not permit random cross-validation.
- A seasonal baseline is not automatically strong.
- A candidate can win overall while losing a fold.
- Smaller point error does not imply calibrated intervals.
- A nonsignificant residual diagnostic does not prove adequacy.
- Reporting percentage is not automatically an admissions denominator.
- A jurisdiction aggregate is not a stable hospital process.
- Testing permission is not deployment permission.

## Scoring

Score the 10-point rubric only after all 14 gates pass. Return a polished submission if any fit sees future data, if targets differ across models, or if the recommendation expands into real operations.
