# Workflow and patient consequences

## Workflow moment and staff voice

The candidate appears after required information is available and before encounter close. The clinician may review, defer, dismiss, or take no action. A passive contextual panel is less interruptive than an active banner, but the synthetic comparison does not establish local burden or preference.

Staff must be able to report repeat work, confusing suppression, unavailable inputs, delays, terminology problems, version mismatch, silence, accessibility failure, patient questions, and hidden follow-up work without blame. Silence from staff is not agreement. The patient-safety owner receives failure concerns; the workflow owner receives burden and fit concerns; the governance council owns pause and stop decisions.

## Patient consequences

Potential benefit is an opportunity for a clinician to consider appropriate confirmatory testing. Potential harms include unnecessary testing, missed testing, delayed care, confusion, language or disability access failure, transportation or cost burden, privacy concern, and inequitable exclusion.

Historical subgroup results begin with support and suppression rules. They do not establish local fairness, a group trait, or permission for group-specific thresholds or actions. Unsupported estimates remain blank.

## Override and nonaction

The clinician retains review and override authority. Dismissal does not prove alert fatigue. No action does not prove that the patient is low risk. A card does not diagnose or order. The patient needs a route to ask questions and correct context in any future locally approved design.

## Current conclusion

The workflow and patient-consequence record supports revision and local co-design. It does not support silent-mode approval, clinical activation, or burden claims about a real service.
