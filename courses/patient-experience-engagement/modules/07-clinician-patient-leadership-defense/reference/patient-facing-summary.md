# Draft summary for patient and caregiver review

## What this teaching project examined

The project asks whether a hospital service and its patient and caregiver advisors should prepare a small, carefully reviewed test of discharge information and feedback. The aim would be to learn whether people understand where to get help and which warning signs matter after they go home, while avoiding extra burden or unwanted contact.

## What information we used

The project used public health survey and healthcare-use data. It also used generated responses and comments so learners could practice checking missing voices, coding text, and comparing methods without using private patient records. The generated comments are not things real patients said.

## What we know and do not know

The public information helps define questions about access, communication, service channels, response, and missing information. It does not tell us what local patients want, whether the proposed process would work, or whether it would improve care. Group differences in the teaching data raise questions but do not explain causes or prove unfair treatment.

## What the team is considering

The draft proposes offering every eligible person a choice of phone, mail, web, interpreter support, proxy support, accessible formats, or no contact. No model would select who receives the offer. The team would record burden, unwanted contact, access failures, safety routing, response, item missingness, and whether results were returned on time.

## What patients and caregivers control

A named patient or caregiver co-lead must review this draft before it is used. That person can change the language, add questions, retain disagreement, ask for a narrower plan, or reject the proposal. Participation, contact, attribution, and recording would require direct choices.

## What happens next

The current recommendation is to revise the proposal before any test. If a later review approves a local protocol, the team must report what changed, what did not change, why, who owns the next step, and what remains open. This draft does not authorize contact or a change in care.
