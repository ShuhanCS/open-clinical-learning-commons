# Leadership defense

- Defense status: `adequate for curriculum construction`.
- Live or equivalent defense: `pending final checkpoint`.

## Q01. What decision is this package asking leaders to make?

It asks whether one service and its patient advisory group should stop, refer, revise, or prepare a bounded prospective measurement and improvement test. It also requires named ownership, a patient-facing return of results, and prespecified reasons to pause or stop. It does not ask whether a model should be deployed or whether the teaching results prove benefit.

## Q02. What are the strongest and weakest parts of the evidence?

The strongest parts are reproducible measurement, response, linkage, qualitative, group-support, and model records from accepted checkpoints. The weakest part is local meaning: there is no confirmed patient/caregiver co-lead, approved local population, workflow, capacity, access test, prospective collection, or governance decision. That gap is why the recommendation is revise before testing.

## Q03. Whose voice is missing, and what changes when that voice joins?

Local patients and caregivers have not interpreted the evidence or approved the proposal. Generated comments and a simulated partnership record cannot fill that role. A named and compensated co-lead can change the language, questions, burden limit, routes, feedback interval, authority, recommendation, or decision to proceed. Their disagreement must remain visible until they agree it is resolved.

## Q04. Why can the accepted HCAHPS items not be treated as a complete local measure?

Q22 and Q23 retain exact teaching wording, scoring, missingness, rights, language, burden, and official-reporting boundaries. They cover a limited discharge-information construct and do not establish understanding, access, acceptability, safety, or benefit. Local clarity or burden items must remain labeled as proposed questions unless separately validated, and teaching results cannot be called official adjusted HCAHPS reporting.

## Q05. What does the linked denominator support and not support?

The 28,455 linked target events describe healthcare use under the accepted source contract and reconcile to the target people, including 12 inpatient stays crossing the source-year boundary. They support service-use questions and denominator checking. They do not establish portal preference, engagement, local workflow, or causal effects. Telehealth is a service channel, and the 45 valid provider-language records have limited support.

## Q06. How should the generated comments be used?

The 420 comments support practice with an eight-theme codebook, a 120-record simulated human benchmark, 80.00000000 percent agreement, kappa 0.77142857, and phrase-rule accuracy 0.78333333. They are not patient testimony, prevalence evidence, or quotations. Human review remains required, and the assisted method cannot decide who receives an offer or what patients value.

## Q07. What do supported group estimates and contrasts mean?

Thirty-five of 52 estimates and 19 of 36 contrasts pass the prespecified support rule. A supported result permits careful review of a question with its count and uncertainty. It does not prove inequity, fairness, discrimination, preference, cause, or a group trait. Unsupported values stay blank, source categories stay intact, and neither supported nor unsupported results are used for patient or group targeting.

## Q08. What is the current patient-partnership status?

It is a simulated construction example with zero actual patient or caregiver statements. The patient/caregiver co-lead is pending before alpha, along with compensation, preparation, access, schedule, attribution, recording choice, and direct review. The current package can define the required authority but cannot claim partnership approval or use the simulated record as evidence of what patients want.

## Q09. Why is the offer universal?

A universal offer protects the decision from using response prediction or group identity to select patients. Every locally eligible discharge would receive approved phone, mail, web, interpreter-supported, proxy-supported, accessible-format, and no-contact choices. Universal offer does not itself prove equal access, so availability, choice, burden, unwanted contact, missingness, and failures are monitored and reviewed with the patient co-lead.

## Q10. Why does the machine-learning result not change the response decision?

The transparent and bounded-ML teaching-composite absolute biases are 2.48289986 and 2.39922466 percentage points. The 0.08367520-point difference is below the prespecified 0.50 threshold. The random forest therefore adds complexity without enough decision improvement. The transparent benchmark remains, the limitation stays visible, and no model selects patients, groups, or actions.

## Q11. What must be learned locally before any test?

The service must define eligibility, owners, staffing, timing, language and disability access, interpreter and proxy capacity, contact choices, safe question routing, privacy, governance, prospective collection, comparison, and review cadence. It must walk through ordinary, after-hours, declined-contact, unavailable-route, and escalation cases. Direct patient partnership and a successful run-in are required before later shared authority could approve a bounded test.

## Q12. How will patients and caregivers know what happened?

Each review cycle must return an accessible plain-language response through requested routes. It states what changed, what did not change, why, who owns the next step, and what remains open. Requests and disagreements stay in a tracked decision record. Missing the agreed return date is an accountability failure that stops progression until an owner, response, and credible capacity plan are restored.

## Q13. When must the proposal stop or change?

It stops or pauses for unsafe routing, unwanted contact, missing access routes, partner rejection or withdrawal, invalid denominators, insufficient completeness, unsupported group claims, privacy or consent failure, excess burden, capacity failure, official-reporting confusion, targeting, or lost ownership. Restart requires specific corrective evidence and shared authority, not merely a promise that the problem was noted.

## Q14. What did agents do, and why can the package pass while the proposal still needs revision?

Agents helped structure records, retrieve accepted evidence, and implement consistency checks without receiving patient or restricted data. Named humans retain clinical, partnership, governance, and verification authority. The package passes because it is reproducible, transparent, scored, and explicit about conditions. The proposal remains `revise before testing` because curriculum quality cannot substitute for local evidence, direct partnership, safe workflow, or authorization.
