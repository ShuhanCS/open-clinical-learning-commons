# Instructor notes

## Teaching sequence

1. Start with the data-class boundary. Every comment, channel, access offer, coder record, and return decision is synthetic.
2. Have learners read the codebook and independently code the fixed 120-comment audit sample before showing agreement results.
3. Preserve coder disagreement. Adjudication adds a decision; it does not rewrite the original codes.
4. Run the keyword classifier only after the human benchmark exists. Use its weak warning-sign and after-hours recall to discuss failure, not to tune a hidden model.
5. Prespecify all groups, measures, references, and support rules before opening the estimate and contrast tables.
6. Ask why unsupported Asian, uninsured, other or multiple race, and sparse positive-count results remain visible but blank.
7. End with a patient-partner question and a bounded Module 06 handoff.

## Reference facts

- 782 synthetic opportunities produce 420 received comments.
- The fixed corpus has eight themes, 84 ambiguous comments, and 42 comments with secondary text first.
- The double-coded audit contains 120 records, 96 coder agreements, 80.00000000 percent agreement, and Cohen's kappa 0.77142857.
- The transparent phrase rule has 0.78333333 benchmark accuracy and 0.78333333 macro recall. Every one of its 420 suggestions requires human review.
- The public review has 13 group rows, 52 measure estimates, 35 supported estimates, 36 contrasts, and 19 supported contrasts.
- Portal preference remains unavailable.

## Facilitation limits

Do not call a theme count prevalence or a synthetic comment a quotation. Do not ask learners to explain a group difference with a stereotype. Keep the source category, support status, uncertainty, and unknown mechanism visible.

The reference coding records are simulated curriculum evidence. Named patient, qualitative-methods, equity, accessibility, language-access, survey-methods, privacy, responsible-AI, clinical, and independent reviews remain pending before alpha.
