# Equity and patient-voice memo

## What the evidence records

The public-derived table supports some descriptive group estimates after prespecified suppression. The lower-income teaching group has a delayed-cost estimate 4.02137981 percentage points above the middle-or-high-income reference, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.59188925 to 7.45087037. The same teaching group has any linked telehealth use 6.88053616 percentage points below the reference, with a confidence interval from -12.44361714 to -1.31745518. These results record service and access fields. They do not explain why the differences appear.

The 420 comments are generated examples. Their theme counts show how a codebook, disagreement record, and assisted-coding audit work. They are not patient testimony or prevalence evidence.

## What remains unknown

The source does not measure discrimination, care offer, need, preference, trust, burden, digital access, portal preference, or the mechanism behind a group difference. Sparse groups remain visible, but unsupported estimates stay blank.

## Question for patient partners

Could a multilingual discharge-feedback process create new cost, channel, disability-access, or after-hours burdens, and what non-digital alternatives and decision rights would make the process worth testing?
