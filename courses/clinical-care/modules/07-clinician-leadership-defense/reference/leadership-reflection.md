# Leadership reflection

The leadership task is to protect the decision boundary while making the next action clear. The accepted analysis keeps a clinical question alive, but it does not supply local workflow, capacity, patient-choice, or authorization evidence.

I would bring the case to a care-improvement council as `revise before testing`. I would ask the council to name the clinical and operational owners, involve a patient or caregiver partner, define a universal-offer workflow, and approve the prospective data contract before considering a live test.

The hardest tradeoff is between learning quickly and adding burden to patients and staff. That tradeoff cannot be settled by the synthetic outcome analysis or by a model score. It requires local capacity evidence, patient choice, safety monitoring, and authority to pause or stop.

I remain accountable for tracing claims, stating uncertainty, and correcting the package. A medical director or council remains accountable for clinical authorization. An agent or model has no decision authority.
