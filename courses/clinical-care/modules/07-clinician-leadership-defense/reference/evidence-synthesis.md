# Evidence synthesis

## Council question

Should the organization prepare a bounded prospective test of a universal discharge follow-up scheduling offer, revise the proposal, refer it, or stop?

## Strongest accepted evidence

The day-30 landmark cohort contains 476 people and 87 acute-return events. Recorded scheduled follow-up is present for 129 people and absent for 347. The accepted packages preserve exact count-first survival, risk-adjusted, site-pattern, subgroup-support, pathway, and model-comparison evidence.

The transparent held-out benchmark has Brier score 0.09609243 and AUC 0.66363212. The bounded random forest has Brier score 0.10745654 and AUC 0.62371615. At the educational threshold, the transparent benchmark flags 25 people with weighted error cost 44; the random forest flags 60 with weighted cost 67. Machine learning does not change the improvement decision.

## Evidence that narrows the decision

The proportional-hazards screen p-value is 0.00636020 and fails the declared screen. One constant hazard ratio cannot lead the survival interpretation. Risk adjustment does not remove residual confounding. The site extension is synthetic and known by construction. Subgroup evidence is sparse and suppressed when support fails.

Offer, preference, acceptance, appointment status, completion, barriers, and burden are not observed. The source is synthetic, so no result establishes real prevalence, effect, quality, access, equity, site performance, safety, or clinical value.

## Leadership conclusion

The evidence supports retaining the question and preparing local prospective measurement. It does not support a live workflow change, model targeting, or a claim that scheduled follow-up reduces acute returns. The recommendation is `revise before testing`.
