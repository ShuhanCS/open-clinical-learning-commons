# People, equity, and safety review

## People affected

Patients and caregivers may gain help arranging follow-up, but they may also carry added calls, travel, technology, language, scheduling, or appointment burden. Discharge clinicians, nurses, care managers, schedulers, primary care practices, and improvement staff carry workflow and documentation work.

## People who may be missed

The retrospective source does not observe offer, preference, acceptance, barriers, burden, or complete appointment state. People without stable contact routes, transportation, digital access, English fluency, flexible work, caregiver support, or a reachable follow-up practice may be missed by the proposed workflow.

## Equity requirements

The test proposal must report eligibility, offer, preference, acceptance, scheduling, and completion counts before rates. It must show missingness and uncertainty for prespecified age, source-recorded gender, race, and ethnicity groups. Small groups remain separate. Unsupported estimates remain suppressed. Group-specific thresholds and rankings are prohibited.

## Safety and unintended consequences

Potential harms include discharge delay, duplicate work, inaccurate appointment status, pressure to accept an appointment, diversion of limited slots, unequal access to preferred formats, privacy exposure, and false confidence from incomplete data. A serious safety concern, unauthorized targeting, or loss of required oversight triggers immediate pause and review.

## Patient partnership

A patient or caregiver partner must review the offer language, choice and refusal route, burden measures, access supports, and interpretation of completion before local authorization.
