# Technical defense

- Defense status: `adequate for curriculum construction`.

## Q01. What exact council decision does this package support?

It supports deciding whether the organization should prepare a bounded prospective test, revise the proposal, refer it, or stop. The reference answer is `revise before testing`.

## Q02. Which evidence is strongest, and which condition most narrows it?

The strongest evidence is the exact accepted cohort, survival, adjustment, variation, equity, pathway, and held-out model package. The synthetic source and absence of prospective offer, preference, appointment, completion, barrier, and burden states most narrow the decision.

## Q03. Why does the failed proportional-hazards screen change the survival summary?

The screen p-value is 0.00636020. A single constant hazard ratio cannot lead the interpretation, so the case leads with counts, Kaplan-Meier evidence, fixed-time evidence, and the failed-screen condition.

## Q04. What does risk adjustment support, and what remains confounded?

It supports a conditional comparison under the declared synthetic model and observed features. It does not remove unmeasured confounding, create exchangeability, or establish a treatment effect.

## Q05. Why does machine learning not change the recommendation?

The bounded random forest has worse held-out Brier and AUC values, flags 60 rather than 25 people, and has weighted error cost 67 rather than 44. The comparison has only 17 evaluation events and establishes no clinical utility.

## Q06. Who may benefit, carry burden, or be missed?

Patients and caregivers may gain scheduling support but may carry calls, travel, technology, language, time, and appointment burden. Staff carry workflow work. People without reachable contact, flexible schedules, transport, language access, technology, caregiver help, or appointment capacity may be missed.

## Q07. Why use universal offer and measurement rather than patient targeting?

The accepted evidence does not establish clinical utility for either model, and group-specific action is unsupported. Universal offer preserves choice and lets the organization measure reach, acceptance, access, workload, and completion without score-based exclusion.

## Q08. What local workflow and capacity evidence is missing?

The package lacks local eligibility, discharge volume, available slots, scheduling ownership, staff time, exception routes, language and disability support, preferred formats, patient-partner review, and approved governance.

## Q09. Which measures show whether the test is delivered as intended?

Offer, preference, appointment-state, workflow-fidelity, and data-completeness measures show delivery. Acute return is an outcome measure. Discharge delay and workload are balancing measures. Offer and completion by supported groups are access measures.

## Q10. What would make you pause, stop, roll back, or refer the work?

A serious safety concern, lost oversight, capacity breach, invalid denominator data, access burden, unauthorized targeting, privacy concern, or protocol mismatch triggers the corresponding rule in `stop-escalation-rules.csv`.

## Q11. What did an agent contribute, and how was it verified?

Codex drafted the package structure, records, measures, and validation logic. Accepted release fingerprints, nested validators, deterministic builds, exact arithmetic, source-page checks, and human curriculum direction provide different verification routes.

## Q12. Why can the package pass while the recommendation remains revise before testing?

The score evaluates whether the package is correct, reproducible, clinically sound, action-guiding, accessible, and accountable. A careful decision to revise is a successful result when the evidence does not justify authorization.
