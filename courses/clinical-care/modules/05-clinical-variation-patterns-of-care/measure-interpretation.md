# Measure interpretation

## Denominators and time

The day-30 pathway proportion uses 129 people with a qualifying scheduled encounter over all 476 landmark-eligible people. Post-landmark person-level proportions use all people in the stated comparison group. Utilization rates use the exact window length: 60 days for days 31 through 90, 90 days for days 91 through 180, and 185 days for days 181 through 365.

The scheduled-follow-up group has 148 scheduled records during days 31 through 90, or 19.12144703 per 1,000 person-days. The no-recorded-follow-up group has 307, or 14.74543708 per 1,000 person-days. Raw counts alone would reverse the comparison because the groups contain 129 and 347 people.

## Treatment records are not adherence

The source has 742 medication rows during days 31 through 365, covering 279 people. These rows support a recorded treatment-exposure measure. They do not establish dispensing at a known time, possession, administration, ingestion, correct dose, persistence, benefit, or patient preference. No medication possession ratio or adherence estimate is calculated.

## Procedure records need clinical composition

The source has 1,832 post-landmark procedure rows covering 297 people. Common descriptions include fetal-heart auscultation, uterine fundal-height evaluation, medication reconciliation, depression screening, and examination after birth. The exposure groups therefore contain different clinical pathways and record intensity. A difference in any-procedure recording cannot be called quality, appropriateness, overuse, or underuse.

## Outcome and case mix

The 87 later acute returns and the Module 04 expected probabilities remain unchanged. The inpatient-versus-emergency outcome difference is interpreted as evidence of clinical composition and possible confounding by need. Module 05 does not refit the expected-outcome model or turn the difference into an index-class effect.

## Clinical and statistical evidence

The curriculum threshold asks whether a process-record difference is large enough to matter operationally in a teaching decision. The interval and p-value describe compatibility and precision under their assumptions. A threshold does not repair bias, and a p-value does not measure clinical importance, measurement validity, or causality.
