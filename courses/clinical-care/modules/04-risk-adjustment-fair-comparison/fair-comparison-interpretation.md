# Fair-comparison interpretation

The teaching-site labels are a deterministic synthetic extension. Their known direct effect on the outcome is zero. Differences among their observed and standardized rates arise from the frozen synthetic records, case mix, assignment construction, and random variation. They are not evidence about a real hospital or clinician.

Expected events come from baseline predicted probabilities. An observed-to-expected ratio compares a count with that model expectation. The standardized rate rescales that ratio to the cohort event rate. The secondary exposure odds ratio compares adjusted odds. None of these quantities is a hazard ratio, and the odds ratio is not a risk ratio.

Residual confounding remains because adjustment does not remove unmeasured confounding. It does not certify fairness, justify a league table, or make interval overlap an equivalence test. Gender, race, and ethnicity were reserved for the later equity audit because this small release had no approved rationale for using them in expected outcomes. That exclusion is not evidence that the fields are irrelevant or that the model is fair.

All six sites are reported with caution. The right use is to decide what variation needs study in Module 05, while preserving uncertainty, support, provenance, and the prohibition on real-site ranking.
