# Survival interpretation

The accepted cohort has 476 synthetic people at the day-30 landmark. There are 129 people with a recorded scheduled follow-up encounter and 347 without one. The analysis observes 87 first later acute returns and administratively censors 389 people at day 335 after the landmark.

At day 30, event-free probability is 0.99224806 in the scheduled-follow-up group and 0.97982709 in the no-recorded-follow-up group, a difference of 0.01242097. At day 180, the estimates are 0.87596899 and 0.91066282, a difference of -0.03469383. At day 335, they are 0.80620155 and 0.82132565, a difference of -0.01512410. The direction changes over time, and every comparison is descriptive.

The log-rank chi-square is 0.17859356 with p = 0.67258471. This does not establish equivalence or prove that follow-up has no clinically relevant association. The unadjusted Cox hazard ratio is 1.10542457 with a 95 percent interval from 0.69479700 to 1.75873453. It is an instantaneous hazard comparison, not a probability, risk ratio, or adjusted estimate. It is not a causal effect.

The proportional-hazards screen fails with p = 0.00636020. The main evidence is therefore the count-first Kaplan-Meier table and fixed-time estimates, not one constant hazard ratio. Module 04 may examine case mix and a time-varying or alternative survival summary without changing this risk set.
