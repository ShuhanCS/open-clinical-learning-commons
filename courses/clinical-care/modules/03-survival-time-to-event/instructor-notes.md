# Instructor notes

## Teaching center

The useful tension is between a routine null-looking group comparison and a failed proportional-hazards screen. Ask learners what each quantity measures before discussing p-values. The correct response is not to hunt for significance. It is to show when the group pattern changes and state why one constant hazard ratio is a weak summary.

## Required corrections

- A censored person is not an event-free person forever. The person contributes known time until censoring.
- A hazard ratio is not a probability, risk ratio, survival probability, or causal effect.
- A p-value of 0.67258471 is not proof of equivalence.
- The day-30 direction differs from the day-180 direction.
- Three later deaths occur after the first later acute return and do not become pre-event censors.
- Zero observed pre-event deaths does not prove noninformative censoring.
- The scheduled encounter is a record, not proof of access, completion, quality, or benefit.

## Progression

Return any package that changes the risk set, hides the PH result, drops censored rows, makes a causal claim, or claims R execution without evidence. A learner may proceed with conditions when fixed-time evidence leads the interpretation and Module 04 receives the unresolved survival-methods referral.
