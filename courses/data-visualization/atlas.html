Foundation

Build data you can trust, then choose and defend analytics that fit the decision.

2 required courses
FND-1 / Foundation Healthcare Data Foundations Build, check, describe, and hand off a healthcare dataset another analyst can trust. 3 creditsNo prerequisite

Seven-week path

  1. Set up a reproducible workspace.
  2. Work with databases and retrieve data.
  3. Build cohorts and analytic tables.
  4. Clean and profile healthcare data.
  5. Produce descriptive results.
  6. Create accessible charts and read time-indexed data.
  7. Complete a reproducible handoff and AI audit.

Final work: SQL, notebook, README, version tag, and data brief in one reproducible toolkit.

FND-2 / Foundation Modeling, Inference, and Reproducible Analytics Turn checked data into analytic evidence and defend the method, assumptions, claims, and monitoring plan. 3 creditsPrerequisite: FND-1

Seven-week path

  1. Define analytic aims and a reproducible modeling workspace.
  2. Fit and interpret regression models.
  3. Build and evaluate prediction workflows.
  4. Handle adjustment, missing data, and longitudinal structure.
  5. Forecast and validate across time.
  6. Test agent-assisted modeling work.
  7. Defend model cards and governance decisions.

Final work: A model or agent-assisted analytics package with a model card.

Applied healthcare decisions

Use the same foundation skills across care, patient experience, performance, decision support, population health, research, and strategy. The capstone path requires six of these seven courses.

7 domain courses
APP-1 / Applied Data for Clinical Care Follow a clinical cohort over time, compare outcomes fairly, and recommend a feasible care-pathway improvement. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Frame a care-pathway decision.
  2. Build longitudinal cohorts and follow-up.
  3. Analyze survival and time-to-event outcomes.
  4. Use risk adjustment for fair comparison.
  5. Study clinical variation and patterns of care.
  6. Connect equity, variation, and a feasible improvement.
  7. Make an integrated clinical recommendation.

Final work: A clinical care improvement brief with an evaluation plan.

APP-2 / Applied Data for Patient Experience and Engagement Treat what patients report about health, access, communication, and trust as evidence for improving care. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Frame a patient-experience and engagement decision.
  2. Work with patient-reported measurement.
  3. Examine response, representation, and bias.
  4. Build linked patient evidence.
  5. Study patient voice, group differences, and equity.
  6. Design a patient-partnered improvement.
  7. Make the recommendation and define accountability.

Final work: A patient-experience and engagement evidence package.

APP-3 / Applied Data for Clinical Performance and Improvement Turn harm, delay, unreliable care, access, or capacity problems into measures and tested improvement decisions. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Frame a clinical performance problem.
  2. Define measures and operational metrics.
  3. Find variation, safety signals, and bottlenecks.
  4. Forecast demand and plan capacity.
  5. Test improvement scenarios and evaluation designs.
  6. Set feasibility, equity, and monitoring requirements.
  7. Defend a performance recommendation.

Final work: A clinical performance improvement package with monitoring and fallback thresholds.

APP-4 / Applied Data for Clinical Decision Support Take a prediction into a clinical workflow, test its burden and failure modes, and stop before it does harm. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Frame a decision support use case.
  2. Define decision logic, triggers, and data.
  3. Assess evidence, calibration, and validation.
  4. Measure alert burden, human factors, and equity.
  5. Build a sandbox prototype and test failure modes.
  6. Write the safety case, monitoring, and governance plan.
  7. Defend the product brief.

Final work: A CDS product brief, sandbox prototype, evaluation plan, and safety case.

APP-5 / Applied Data for Population Health and Equity Measure how health differs across groups and places, then turn that picture into an accountable intervention plan. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Frame a population-health decision.
  2. Build population measures from linked data.
  3. Measure disparities and state data limits.
  4. Use place-based evidence and geographic reasoning.
  5. Evaluate targeting and fairness.
  6. Design an accountable intervention plan.
  7. Defend the population recommendation.

Final work: A population intervention analytics plan with an equity rationale and monitoring measures.

APP-6 / Applied Data for Health Research and Innovation Design a health study that can answer a causal question and produce evidence another researcher can rerun and review. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Move from a question to a causal estimand and study design.
  2. Test data feasibility and measurement.
  3. Use causal diagrams for confounding and identification.
  4. Run estimation, adjustment, and sensitivity analysis.
  5. Preregister reproducible protocol analytics.
  6. Evaluate innovation evidence and create a dissemination artifact.
  7. Meet reporting standards and defend the method.

Final work: A protocol, feasibility analysis, innovation evidence, dissemination artifact, and methodological defense.

APP-7 / Applied Data for Health Systems Strategy, Finance, and Value Turn service, cost, finance, and value data into a strategic investment recommendation leadership can weigh. 3 creditsPrerequisites: FND-1 and FND-2

Seven-week path

  1. Frame the strategic decision.
  2. Read finance and value data.
  3. Diagnose service-line performance and access.
  4. Assess the strategic context.
  5. Build financial and value models.
  6. Compare and prioritize options.
  7. Deliver the executive decision package.

Final work: A board-ready strategic investment decision package and monitoring plan.

Capstone

Approve a feasible proposal, then deliver one complete healthcare analytics project for a real decision owner.

Preparation + final project
CAP-0 / Capstone preparation Capstone Preparation Define the problem, prove the data and methods are feasible, clear ethics, and earn approval before the project begins. 0 credits / Pass-FailFND-1, FND-2, and at least 5 applied courses

Seven-week path

  1. Frame the problem.
  2. Identify stakeholders and the analytic aim.
  3. Confirm data feasibility.
  4. Write the methods plan.
  5. Resolve ethics and privacy requirements.
  6. Build the reproducible project skeleton and timeline.
  7. Defend the proposal.

Final work: An approved capstone proposal with data access, methods, ethics, and delivery plans.

CAP-1 / Capstone Capstone: Learning Health System Analytics Run one complete healthcare analytics project from an authentic decision through a defended recommendation. 3 creditsFND-1, FND-2, 6 applied courses, and CAP-0

Seven-week path

  1. Scope the decision with stakeholders.
  2. Acquire data and build the cohort.
  3. Select methods and run the analysis.
  4. Evaluate validity and possible harm.
  5. Plan implementation and evaluation.
  6. Prepare communication and independent review.
  7. Complete the oral defense and define the next learning cycle.

Final work: A reproducible final package, oral defense, implementation plan, and reflection.

04

Rebuild it in R

Know what you are making and why before you run the code. Then use the Teaching Key to check your result.

What you are making

A four-panel view of emergency department flow

Plot the monthly mean, median, 90th percentile, and share of visits over eight hours. The lab also creates three earlier charts that help you discover why all four measures are needed.

Why this visualization

One average hides two care pathways

The mean looks stable because shorter discharged visits offset longer admitted visits. The four-panel view keeps the improvement visible while exposing the boarding problem in the upper tail.

Teaching Key: what a correct answer looks like

These four trends belong together. The first two describe the center of the distribution. The last two show the patients who waited longest.

Model chart title

Typical emergency department visits improve while the longest stays worsen.

Model clinical decision

Continue the fast-track work for discharged patients. Ask patient-flow and bed-management teams to investigate boarding and monitor the upper tail.

Why this earns credit

The chart preserves the familiar mean and median, adds measures of long waits, uses honest scales, names the unit, and connects the pattern to a healthcare decision.

What not to claim

This synthetic dataset does not prove that fast track caused the improvement, that boarding caused harm, or that any real hospital had these results.
