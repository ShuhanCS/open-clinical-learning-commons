# People, equity, safety, and workforce review

## People who may be affected

- Adults arriving to the fictional emergency service may experience delay, abandonment, routing changes, or different access to care.
- High-acuity patients may face harm if queue or fast-track rules are applied without clinical safeguards.
- People using language or mobility support may face access barriers, but the target-window comparisons are unsupported at 401 and 242 encounters.
- People discharged from the service would need complete follow-up before a 72-hour return measure can be interpreted.
- Nurses, physicians, support staff, schedulers, and operations staff may carry added coverage, interruption, handoff, documentation, or monitoring work.

## Evidence limits

Safety outcome and 72-hour return were not simulated. Workforce interruption and perceived-load baseline are prospectively unavailable. Simulated access gaps and modeled flex hours do not establish real equity, feasibility, productivity, burden, or staffing need.

## Required protections

- Clinical review owns any acuity, safety, routing, or harm concern.
- Access review owns language, mobility, missingness, support, and unavailable comparisons.
- Workforce review owns role, schedule, interruption, overtime, workload, and coverage assumptions.
- Staff concerns must have a blame-free route and a recorded response.
- A small or unsupported group cannot be targeted or described through a favorable or unfavorable estimate.
- No unavailable value may be converted to zero, safe, favorable, or not applicable.

## Decision effect

These gaps require revision before testing. They are not minor documentation tasks and cannot be closed by the analytic package alone.
